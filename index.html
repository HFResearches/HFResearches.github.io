Our purpose

Evidence before confidence.

Our purpose is to move quantitative research forward by combining rigorous statistical reasoning with a deep understanding of the systems that execute it. A model is not complete when the equation works—it must also survive real data, real costs, and real computational constraints.

01

Statistical discipline

We examine assumptions, uncertainty, out-of-sample behavior, and the possibility that an apparent edge is only noise.

02

Systems awareness

We study latency, memory, concurrency, networking, and execution because implementation details can change a strategy’s real behavior.

03

Transparent validation

Research should be inspectable. Methods, benchmarks, limitations, and failures matter as much as the final result.

Research method

From hypothesis to measured system.

Every project begins with a falsifiable question and ends with evidence that another researcher can inspect. The goal is not to manufacture impressive results; it is to understand what remains true after serious testing.

  1. Define

    State the market hypothesis, assumptions, measurable outcome, and conditions that would invalidate the idea.

  2. Engineer

    Build the data and execution path in modern C++, paying attention to ownership, concurrency, memory layout, networking, and failure handling.

  3. Validate

    Measure distributions, risk, costs, sensitivity, and out-of-sample performance instead of relying on a single return number.

  4. Publish

    Share the code, methodology, benchmarks, assumptions, and known limitations so the work can be examined and improved.

Research foundations

What we work on.

HFResearches treats quantitative finance as both a mathematical and an engineering problem. Our work connects research logic to the infrastructure responsible for carrying it into reality.

Market data systems

Reliable ingestion, normalization, storage, and processing of real-time and historical financial data.

Strategy research

Hypothesis-driven studies of market behavior using probability, simulation, backtesting, and robust evaluation.

Execution engineering

Research into latency, slippage, transaction costs, networking, and the mechanics that separate theoretical results from live execution.

Performance measurement

Reproducible benchmarks that expose trade-offs in algorithms, concurrency models, memory use, and system architecture.
